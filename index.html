Verification and synthesis. One system.

We prove your RTL. We build its controller.

One tool that both proves a chip design is correct and builds the control logic it’s missing — delivered as an API you can drop into a product or an AI coding loop.

Technical detail

Mununu is a Rust engine that listens to reactive systems — hardware RTL, protocols, the quiet choreography of agentic-AI workflows — and asks the temporal questions that decide whether they hold. In the grammar of μ-calculus and LTL, over Compositional Labeled Transition Systems, it proves what is true and builds, correct by construction, the controllers that keep it true. It meets you as a CLI, an HTTP API, and a web UI.

The reason this system exists

Two gaps in correctness. Both are closed.

Two problems hold hardware teams back today: the tool that verifies chips is expensive and can’t be embedded in a product, and the hardest correctness questions can’t even be written down. Mununu closes both.

gap 01

The oracle gap

The instrument that judges whether RTL is correct costs a hundred to five hundred thousand dollars a seat, every year — and it will not live inside your product, nor ride along in an agent’s loop. If your system needs a conscience of correctness at its core, that price and that posture quietly close the door.

The expressiveness gap

Whole families of correctness — the power to recover, to survive a reset, to never deadlock, to always answer — cannot even be spoken in SVA or LTL. And synthesis, which builds a controller rather than merely grading one, is offered by no commercial or SaaS vendor at all.

We close both. Sound, expressive, embeddable — we verify and we build.

The defining property

“Can it always recover?”

The most valuable safety question is easy to ask and, until now, has been impossible to answer: from any state a design lands in, can it always get back to a safe one? Mununu answers it — proven, on real chips.

AG EF good From wherever the design may wander, is there still a road home — to something good, safe, or at rest?
Technical detail

This asks after the shape of a design’s state space, not the fortunes of any single run. A trace-based assertion watches one path at a time; “recovery remains possible from everywhere” quantifies over the branching futures of every reachable state. That makes it branching-time — a thing SystemVerilog Assertions and LTL bounded model checkers cannot even utter, no matter how long they run.

Mununu decides it soundly, and has shown it on real OpenTitan silicon RTL — the same recoverability question answered, definitively, in both directions on a production security state machine.

Soundness by design

Three verdicts. No bluffing.

When a verification tool says “pass,” can you trust it? Mununu gives one of three honest answers — yes, no, or “I can’t tell yet” — and never fakes confidence it doesn’t have.

Technical detail

Every question returns True, False, or ⊥ — undecided. When abstraction can no longer carry a definite answer, Mununu falls silent rather than guess. Honest scale today is block and FSM level, an exact cone of roughly 40 bits; beyond it, ⊥ — never a false “pass.”

Beneath it runs a portfolio that takes the first definite answer and raises an alarm the moment two sound engines disagree:

The science

Machine-checked. Reproducible.

The claims here aren’t marketing. The core guarantees are checked by machine, and every result ships with the evidence to reproduce it byte for byte.

Technical detail

The core soundness theorems are mechanized in Lean 4 and Isabelle/HOL. Definite verdicts carry to the real system at every alternation depth (Bruns–Godefroid, 3-valued KMTS); synthesis rests on memoryless winning strategies (Zielonka), ranking certificates (Podelski–Rybalchenko), GR(1) (Piterman–Pnueli–Sá’ar), and supervisory control (Ramadge–Wonham). Every verified example ships with a manifest, its source, and a byte-deterministic transcript, and every verdict maps to a CI exit code.
